I woke up normally on 13 November 2026 as I do most days. I often check my phone before I flip the lid of my laptop open. My emails on Outlook were stuck in a perpatual loading state. I thought little of it. I powered on my laptop while I began properly powering myself up with a cup of coffee. The emails on my laptop also were failing to load. In terms of troubleshooting I'd ruled out that it was a device specific issue. It wasn't localised to my phone or laptop. As it would later turn out, it was far from being local. It was global and Microsoft was in the beginning stages of having a horryfing Friday thr 13th.

I'm not the most superstitious of individuals and I'm sure Microsoft is neither but I couldn't help myself make the connection (like many others did online too). News articles were flooding the internet about how businesses across the world were waking up to severly disrupted operations. This flood of articles was a sort of inverse of the lack of emails finding their intended mailbox destinations.

Two thoughts stuck with me during this outage.

Firstly, it seems in recent years there have been several prolific incidents of major outages that span vast regions. In November 2025 a Cloudflare issue affected major companies like Canva, ChatGPT, Spotify, Uber, X, and thousands of other websites globally. Roughly 8.5 millions Windows devices crashed in July 2024 thanks to a Crowdstike fault on those Windows devices, affecting critical industries like airlines, hospitals, banks, emergency systems, and even retail point-of-sales systems. In 2021 Meta managed to "unplug" millions of their own users from Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger due to a faulty router configuration command they rolled out. These are some of the noteworthy ones in recent memory. I've pondered over this. Are global outages becoming more frequent or is it more a case that incresingly much more of our daily systems are becoming globally interconnected.

Secondly, I noted how things seemed to continue inspite of this global outage. It certainly disrupted normal operations for thousands, if not more, businesses around the world, yet the world didn't stop turning. Those businesses didn't shut up shop and hunker down until Microsoft managed to rectify the issue. They made contingency plans and got on with the job knowing that normal operations would eventually be restored. This isn't to say that they accepted it graciosuly though. Companies pay Microsoft billions of dollars for a premium service. Microsoft will certainly be experiencing a barage of questions to explain themselves (they might hope those emails some of the ones that don't reach their destination).

At the end of my working day the issue had mostly been resolved. Emails were flowing into my company mailbox as per normal. All had been restored. Like many other companies we had weathered the storm. We even managed to share some jokes and memes at Microsoft's expense.

On Saturday, 29 August, two of the world's greatest rugby nations met once more in a closely contested match. The teams were the mighty All Blacks from New Zealand and The Springboks, reigning world champions from my country South Africa. They met in Cape Town at the foot of Table Mountain. The Springboks would go on to beat their arch rivals but that wasn't the main talking point of the spectacle. What really set the internet ablaze was the spectacularly low flyover performed by two Airlink planes over the stadium before the match. The planes cleared the stadium by a mere 41 feet or approximately 12.5 metres. The roar of the plane engines was still reverberating around the stadium before the internet exploded with people giving their two cents and memes started spreading like wildfire. I remember thinking when I first saw it live on TV that it looked quite low. Then all the various angles started popping up. It wasn't quite low. It was ridiculously low. I had two thoughts that seemed at odds with each other. The first was that I felt it was quite a risky maneuver. The second was that I thought it was pretty damn cool!

In the following days the stunt would be widely debated. It was clear that this was a trending topic. That is where a very clever developer or developers decided to capitalize. A mere three days after the flyover happened, a website called [flappylink.com](https://www.mobygames.com/game/267989/flappy-link/) appeared online. It was a simple, fun web game. The entire premise of the game is to keep a plane airborne and do low flyovers over stadiums. And yes, also avoid the pooping hadedas (a bird in South Africa with an incredibly distinct sound). The game art had a pleasing retro feel to it. You control your plane in front of an aesthetically pleasing 16-bit Table Mountain while puffy clouds gently breeze past. It has similar game mechanics to the popular Flappy Bird or the Google Dino Run games. The game's name, Flappy Bird, is even a nod to its predecessor Flappy Bird. It's a clever marriage of the game Flappy Bird and Airlink, the airliner whose planes performed the flyover.

![Flappy Link Iteration 1]({{ "https://nick-gibbens-org.github.io/Blog-Assets/images/flappy-bird-iteration-one.avif" }}){:class="image-border image-max-width-75"}

Beyond the simple gameplay there wasn't much more to the site. It had the ability to turn off the music if it started feeling too repetitive and a leaderboard. A simple but fun game along with a simple API was all it offered. Yet that was enough to spread like wildfire in much the same way the actual event did, at least in the South African landscape. Various news outlets ran online news articles about the game. It showed up across the social media platforms. And people were sharing links to the site with friends and family. The developers followed up quickly with some updates. Now there was more than just one plane in the sky. There could be as many as three. A power-up meant you could operate a second plane. A third plane which you didn't operate lazily flew across the top of the screen dragging an advertisement banner. The developers had found a novel way to monetize the site (I assume the advertisement wasn't for free). A form was added to capture your details if you wanted to advertise on the site. A social media link was added as well as a link to the site's developers. Shout out to [Glucode](https://www.glucode.com/). The most noticeable update was the ability to teleport to Johannesburg and perform flyovers over the FNB Stadium which would be the location of the next match between the All Blacks and Springboks.

![Flappy Link Iteration 2]({{ "https://nick-gibbens-org.github.io/Blog-Assets/images/flappy-link-joburg-setting.avif" }}){:class="image-border image-max-width-75"}

It's been less than a week since tongues were set wagging by the audacious flyover and even shorter since Flappy Link arrived on the scene. The game is riding on the wings of this trending moment in time and is enjoying the heightened interest that comes along with it. There could be yet more updates to come. Time will tell. But before long the interest will wane. This is not a slight at the game or the developers. This is just the way of the world. Before long there will be some other trending topic and people's attention will move on. But while the flyover is still fresh news Flappy Link should still see good traffic to the site. The developers at Glucode only had a very limited timespan to pull this off. Release this game a month from now and it wouldn't have had the same impact as it did releasing it a couple of days after the event took place. The ink on the headlines was still wet when it was released and that is what made all the difference.

The site's development is relatively straightforward. The game mechanics aren't groundbreaking. There are plenty of examples of parallax scrolling games. In the age of AI it would be relatively simple to get a basic parallax scrolling game made in a short time frame (not that I'm saying that is the case here). The beauty of a retro looking game is that you can also have basic imagery. The images and elements used in Flappy Bird are simple but pleasing although I must say the Joburg updates were really impressive. What makes Flappy Bird so impressive isn't so much the game itself but more about how the developers could execute while this event was still so topical. A simple idea that is executed and released to the world beats a brilliant idea that lives in a non-deployed private repository.
